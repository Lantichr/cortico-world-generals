# Engine and S1 architecture

`src/index.ts` exports the `GENERALS` World definition. `definition.ts` supplies defaults and creates the main-process proxy. Game protocol, observations, control and tactical policy stay inside this World; the framework Core and a concrete Persona are not dependencies of the policy.

## Process and control boundary

Autonomous control works on unmodified Cortico 0.2.3. [Host operator-pause integration](host-compatibility.md) is optional; the engine child process does not require it.

The World definition creates `GeneralsWorldProxy`. The proxy forwards tools, console state, events and any host pause notifications to an engine child process that owns the game connection, map, S1 and move executor. S1 remains active while the main model is thinking, handing off context or unavailable. Without pause notifications it also continues during global pause. With notifications, operator pause stops new movement and autonomous start votes, invalidates the current S1 decision and strategic intent, and requests clearing pending moves; resume retains delegation with a new control epoch. A clear request cannot recall actions already processed. Disconnect or engine exit revokes autonomous control; restarting creates a fresh connection without restoring the room, delegation or intent.

Both manual movement and S1 use `agent/executor.ts` for the ordered transport, increasing attack indices, local cursor and pending-arrow ledger. `agent/contracts.ts` separates authoritative observations, decisions, actions, feedback and versioned strategic intents. The S1 policy has no model calls; S2 is an optional role performed by the host model through World tools.

## Decision and search budgets

S1 evaluates new server turns, sends at most one next step and observes feedback before continuing. Decisions identify the game, control epoch and map revision; stale decisions are rejected. `generals_agent_status`, also included in observations, reports `bounded_rule_search`, the last decision, search cost, pending action and last feedback. `agent.decisionBudgetMs` and `agent.maxSearchNodes` bound search; exhausted searches use completed candidates or the disclosed one-step fallback, marked by `lastDecision.search.fallback`. Candidate scores and arrival estimates are policy choices and projections, not observed outcomes.

## Gathering and transport

Field gathering targets must have a route to an external boundary without crossing the general. Troops in closed branches can first return to the general; the general may be a destination and cannot be an intermediate tile. Recovery competes with other economic tasks by delivered troops and route length. Gathering searches owned routes separately, preserving legal detours when a shorter attack route crosses enemy land or the general. A gathering route keeps its target through intermediate merges and ordinary expansion opportunities; arrival completes it, changed ownership or an unusable route invalidates it, and urgent defense, interception or an adjacent enemy general can interrupt it. Every step uses the current map and the general's current departure reserve. Remaining economic search nodes are shared among candidate sources in proportion to the square root of their armies, giving the main army more search coverage.

S1 can combine several sources on a visible owned shortest-path tree. Branches merge before traversing a shared tail, so that tail consumes actions once. Source subsets and branch order share one action deadline, consume intermediate garrisons once and include production. `complete=true` means every selected leg was projected; `searchComplete` and the logged `search_complete` describe the bounded source-subset and two branch-order searches on that tree, without proving global optimality. Economic trees retain their final recipient across changes of carrier, and every new observation reprojects all remaining legs against current facts and the original deadline. Reprojection validates fixed paths without repeating their optimization.

Transport scoring compares reinforcement of the general, a field army and a frontier force by added usable troops and serial move cost. It also tests one intermediate owned recipient when that avoids an exposed route or improves delivered troops per action. Recipient production is excluded from delivered troops, and visible objectives cap useful reinforcement by their shortfall. Without a demonstrated positive objective shortfall, field transport value is capped by the increase over the largest original stack on its paths at the same projected turn; moving an existing main army does not create that army again. Without an active gathering intent, a recipient with an affordable, timing-checked city or general mission can stop waiting for transport; ordinary expansion does not interrupt an existing gathering route. Troops consumed along a transport are excluded from the following objective's garrisons. Home staging requires a demonstrated purpose, including comparison with direct completion; incomplete comparisons add no offensive staging value. If a mission risks interception while home needs no reinforcement, S1 tries another task.

Offensive home staging is valued only when one completed transport plan supplies the full departure and reserve requirement. Closed-branch recovery requires a complete current-map proof that every actual source lacks an external boundary without passing through the general; an incomplete proof adds no recovery value. Unknown obstacle fog and tunnels do not prove a branch is enclosed.

## Defense and arrival estimates

Defense planning considers reachable active enemy forces without a ten-step cutoff. Visible assault estimates retain player and source identities. For each player, nearest-first and largest-first shortest-path sequences share one move clock and a private garrison map; forces merge along their paths and consumed sources cannot be sent again. Each arrival prefix requires the cumulative delivered armies plus one surviving defender. Single-force arrivals remain separate scenarios. Historical locations are alternative single-force hypotheses and are not added to visible sequences or other historical locations. These selected orders do not enumerate every enemy route, split, wait or reinforcement.

Defense projects multiple reinforcement routes on a private map with one shared action deadline, production and consumed source/intermediate garrisons. Attacks resolve before that tick's production; an arrival after `d` moves uses natural production only through `observedTurn + d - 1`. Visible affordable neutral and enemy tiles can shorten a return. Reasons report `defense_sufficient`, `defense_partial` or `defense_unavailable` with `target`, `projected`, `gap`, `serial_moves`, `deadline_turn`, `slack` and `action_required`. Slack is the remaining serial move budget before the estimated enemy arrival. Economic missions must leave enough time and troops for a return or an independent defense; delayed defense routes are checked again for interception. `mission_recalled` identifies a rejected mission, while unavailable projections or exhausted timing searches report uncertainty.

Route exposure uses possible enemy paths through non-wall fog, ignoring intervening resistance and extra enemy reinforcements. A route is rejected if an enemy could defeat its carrier before the next departure. Defense targets ignore friendly screens that the plan may withdraw. One unmoved neighbor of the general can contribute to defense only after a complete separation check proves every known threat must cross it, with no historical threat assumptions; its garrison is counted once against the combined visible enemy budget. Any plan that moves troops out of that screen loses its contribution. Home reinforcement on the arrival turn is counted before the attack; an earlier enemy action can invalidate that estimate. These conditional projections do not guarantee survival, and an unavailable plan does not prove that no defense exists. Only the next step is sent before observing and replanning.

For an unsafe mission or return, and when shortest reinforcement plans are insufficient, S1 can search visible alternate routes within the remaining budget. The search retains at most three short or strong path labels per tile and checks waiting sources and intermediate garrisons before merging them. Waiting checks use observed armies or the most recent post-move, pre-production garrison; later production cannot cover an earlier attack. Longer routes compete by arrival time and delivered armies; label truncation or budget exhaustion leaves the search incomplete. Composite missions check general coverage against every cumulative home-arrival prefix. A mission cannot later spend the general's undamaged projected garrison after a possible attack; it must reobserve before planning that departure. Field-route collisions still check individual enemy sources and do not model cumulative field losses.

An adjacent general capture skips later enemy arrivals only when a one-tick check establishes a terminal 1v1 capture under the observed bounds. It checks enemy reinforcement, full/half attacks on the source and a simultaneous attack on the self general. Unseen neighbors prevent that proof unless public total armies equal all currently visible armies of that player. Otherwise the ordinary mission safety checks apply. The offline arena does not implement the official simultaneous general swap; a dedicated fixture tests that boundary.

An unavailable future garrison can invalidate a full mission while its next step remains safe. Before following an unverified gathering goal, S1 prefers an available, timing-checked one-step land or city capture by that force. `mission_next_step_only` permits only a separately verified first step that leaves no known general shortfall; `goal_unverified` marks the intended destination as unverified. Prefix missions retain the remaining path for current-visible projection and timing checks on the next observation; gathering also rechecks ownership and purpose. A lost source, completed target or unusable path invalidates the continuation, and urgent tasks can interrupt it. Route discovery can reach the general as a destination but does not expand through it from another source, so a forbidden transit shortcut cannot suppress a legal detour. A failed alternate-route search preserves an already established unsafe result.

S1 can reserve troops and prepare reinforcements against a recently hidden or departed enemy stack for up to twelve turns after its last sighting. Repeated imports of the same sighting are deduplicated; a similar visible stack explains a departure only within its elapsed movement distance. `history_guard` in the decision reason records the sighting turn and the assumption that the army may approach the general; `history_assumption` identifies a defense plan based on that estimate. Neither updates the tile's current ownership or armies. Inactive players retain static garrisons and capturable generals but produce no moving threats. Elimination invalidates old general targets; lifecycle changes reset committed routes. Search deadlines are cooperative: route expansion and task scoring check elapsed time, while map scans, selected assault generation and the bounded one-step fallback can add overhead.

## S2 reports

`generals.action` archives sent S1 actions and decisions, and `generals.feedback` archives their later feedback without delivering either into model context. Detailed `generals.agent` status is also archived. After S1 delegation, `generals.strategy` delivers compressed reports at `agent.reportIntervalMs`, with earlier reports on control, intent or player lifecycle changes, results, first general sightings, city-count changes and new defense shortfalls. Pending reports merge into the latest state. Reports contain public scores and dated net changes, compact player statuses, own city count, up to three visible forces per side, dated general sightings, task, conditional estimates and action feedback. They omit the full map, search tree and rationale. Each fact, estimate and outcome retains its own observation turn; historical armies and net score changes do not establish current hidden armies or city count.

The full tool, intent and feedback contract is in [contracts.md](contracts.md). Search predictions never replace the current map or last-seen records, and estimates retain their stated observation turns and assumptions.

## Verification

Offline tests use a local WebSocket server and a deterministic arena for complete S1 games against scripted opponents. They do not connect to the Bot Server or start a real bot.

`tests/agent/arena.ts` is an independent two-player referee with fog and common 2/50-turn production. Its scripted baselines cover stationary, random, expanding, pushing and raiding behavior. Tests vary seeds, mirror starts and use standard and City-State modes. The arena resolves players in alternating order, and does not model live network latency, the official queue or AFK enforcement. Its results establish behavior under those conditions and do not measure human-level playing strength.


FFA uses the same movement and production model. `agent/ffa.ts` selects an attack focus from reachable surviving opponents, with an optional S2 target override. A focus is a heuristic preference; general-capture opportunities and defense against every opponent remain eligible. S1 can develop and scout without selecting an opponent.

Home defense combines cumulative demands from different players at each deadline. Within one player, selected attack orders and historical locations remain alternatives. Across players, the greatest prefix demand of each player is summed; this conservative envelope does not simulate a joint optimal schedule or conflict between enemy routes. The strategy brief labels it `parallel_upper_bound` and lists its players.

FFA briefs retain at most eight players, prioritizing self, the S2 target, S1 focus and nearby threats. Enemy forces include one representative per player for at most six players; omitted counts and full lifecycle totals expose truncation. An eliminated target invalidates its intent. Capturing one opponent does not finish the delegated game; only the server result ends control.

`tests/agent/ffa-arena.ts` is a separate N-player referee with local visibility, production, elimination inheritance and simultaneous reciprocal general captures. Explicit initiative orders exercise contested captures. It does not implement the official dependency resolver, network queues or AFK rules; offline results do not establish competitive strength.
